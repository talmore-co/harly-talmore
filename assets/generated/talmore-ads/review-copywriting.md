# Independent synthetic copywriting review

16 September 2026. Second simulated senior recruitment/direct-response copywriter. This is an AI-generated professional judgment exercise, not a real expert endorsement, audience study or performance forecast. I reviewed the supplied brief, both actual contact sheets, and full export images C2, C3, D2, E2 and F1 through F4. I did not consult other reviewers' findings. All recommendations below are proposals.

## Main recommendation

Lead the next copy exploration with F2's recognizable gaming interests, F1's competitive experience, and a revised F3 hands-on hobby angle. Keep C2 as the plain recruitment comparison and E2 as the training-led comparison. D2 is a useful face-visible alternative.

The central copy problem is the gap between recognizing a hobby and understanding the job. Several ads make the hobby instantly visible while putting employment, location and eligibility in smaller type. The best next version should let someone understand three things immediately: this is a Robot Operator vacancy, the work is onsite in Alabang, and prior interests may be relevant while job training is provided.

Gaming background is preferred. Competitive experience is advantageous. Neither the posting nor the supplied scoring establishes that only gamers, only esports players or only headset owners can apply. Conversely, training does not make every applicant equally aligned with the questionnaire preferences.

Selecting more hobbies is not automatically better under an average-based score. For example, selecting FPS and strategy produces 6.5 for that question, not 13. Advertising should invite truthful experience descriptions, not coach candidates to select answers for points.

## Mobile reading, CTA and factual credibility

The contact sheets show each ad at roughly phone-feed width, making them a useful visual proxy. This was an image inspection, not an in-app usability test. Headline comprehension is generally good. Small supporting copy and the common education line demand deliberate reading. Full-resolution sharpness does not solve small displayed type.

- Put "Robot Operator" and "Onsite in Alabang" close to the main hook. A reader should not need the tiny "We're hiring" label to distinguish a vacancy from a gaming product or VR demonstration.
- Keep "Rotating shifts, including nights" visible. It is more decision-relevant than a second sentence about focus. Do not shorten it to "flexible shifts", which would imply applicant control over hours.
- The education line is long. Consider two lines: "At least 2 years of college" and "or a bachelor's degree". Repeat it in primary text. "Fresh graduates welcome" does not mean every school-leaver qualifies.
- "Apply now" is a clear existing CTA. For image copy, "View job & apply" better describes a job-page destination. Keep any actual platform button choice consistent with the available setup; this is a wording proposal, not a platform capability claim. Remove the additional unlabeled circular arrows in F2/F4 if they compete with the single action.
- Never use "remote job". Say "Control robots remotely from the Alabang facility." The domestic-looking sofa in D2 and desk settings in F2/F3 can otherwise reinforce a work-from-home interpretation.
- Salary is a material omission across the set and the supplied posting. The known fact is minimum wage; the exact rate and pay basis are unknown. State "Pay: minimum wage" in primary text rather than implying premium tech pay. Obtain the precise amount and basis for candidate-facing job details. Do not invent take-home pay, night premiums, allowances or benefits.
- Alabang and rotating nights are disclosed, but candidates still lack the exact reporting location, shift windows and rotation details needed to assess a commute. Request those facts. Do not promise transport, easy commuting or accommodation.
- Training provision is user-confirmed but missing from the supplied posting. Align the destination's explanation with the ad before launch. "Training provided" is supported; "paid training", guaranteed mastery and progression into engineering are not.
- Basic English is acceptable. Primary text can say so plainly without claiming that communication is unimportant. The role includes documentation, escalation and teamwork.

## Generated imagery and credibility

The inspected D2, E2 and F images are plausible at feed size. D2 shows skin texture and loose hair rather than a completely smooth face. E2/F1 show a headset over the eyes, recognizable controllers and wrist straps. F2/F3 show understandable hand positions on a gamepad. I did not observe a conspicuous defect that changes the copy recommendation; that is not verification of hardware accuracy.

The more consequential credibility issue is implied evidence. A believable fictional person at a desk can be mistaken for an actual employee using employer equipment. Avoid captions such as "Meet our operators", "Your workstation" or "A day at our facility". No actual workstation or robot type is confirmed. The stylized illustrations avoid photographic workplace claims, but B1/B3/C1 can suggest a training course and A1/A2/C3 can suggest a controller product.

Headsets may attract attention, but hide eye contact and can make the ad look recreational. D2 provides a sensible face-visible comparison. Keep both male and female representations. Appearance is not evidence of applicant suitability, and no supplied source establishes that an attractive face, headset or gender yields better applicants.

## Evidence and testing interpretation

I relied on the verified source notes supplied in the brief, not a new independent source audit:

- [Meta Blueprint creative diversification training](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/page/705435-ad-creative-that-converts-how-to-diversify-your-ads-for-better-results), listed as 26 March 2026, supports varying messages, visuals and formats. It does not establish that these VR portraits beat face-visible portraits.
- [Akvile DeFazio, Search Engine Land, 26 August 2026](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-creative-diversity-485751), argues for meaningful creative differences. This is practitioner commentary. The reported creative-diversity metric was in development; this review assumes neither availability nor a usable optimization rule.
- [Meta Blueprint creative testing course](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/253050-ad-creative-testing-course), as summarized in the supplied notes, supports A/B and lift testing for measurement. Seventeen ads in one delivery pool would not constitute a fair randomized test by itself.

My copy judgment is to compare materially different reasons to apply: specific game interests, competitive experience, practical hobbies and a clear training-supported vacancy. A male/female image swap is useful representation, but does not by itself test a different employment proposition.

Assess suitable completed applications and downstream recruiting quality. CTR can help diagnose attention, but cannot resolve whether readers misunderstood pay, location or the work. The supplied qualified-application threshold is disabled, so any future QualifiedApplication optimization requires configuration and verification first. Budget, volume and timing are unknown; this report does not prescribe a sample size, test duration or projected lift.

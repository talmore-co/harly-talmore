# Talmore Robot Operator: synthetic creative study

16 September 2026. Seventeen static ad options reviewed after the portrait and questionnaire-led copy revisions.

**Bottom line:** advance F1, F2 and F3 as specific skill-based concepts, retain C2 as a plain vacancy comparison, and E2 as a broader training-led VR comparison. Improve job comprehension and practical-fit information before buying traffic. These are test candidates, not predicted winners.

Canvas: https://design.jointhetroops.com/c/FKQf0gdqBV

## What this study is

One AI session modeled six target-candidate perspectives. Two separate sessions reviewed the same assets independently as a direct-response strategist and a recruitment copywriter. Each received the job facts, questionnaire priorities, current-source notes and all 17 rendered ads. They did not read each other's conclusions. The lead assistant synthesized their reports afterward.

This is synthetic concept screening, not interviews with six people or consultation with two human experts. Separate sessions limit direct cross-influence but share model assumptions; their agreement is not independent statistical evidence. There was no timed exposure, live-feed test or campaign performance data. No CTR, CPA or conversion forecasts are justified.

## What the reviews agree on

### 1. Hobby recognition needs an immediate link to the job

Controllers and headsets can initially suggest games, hardware or a VR experience. Put **Robot Operator · Onsite in Alabang** close to the headline. In primary text, explain: **Operate remote-controlled robots from the Alabang facility.** Describe procedures, monitoring, documentation and escalation using the published duties. Do not invent the actual robot or task type.

### 2. The questionnaire supports more specific invitations

Competitive and former competitive experience score highly, as do FPS, racing, flight sims and VR. RC/drone/controller and technical-project experience provide an additional route beyond esports. F1/F2/F3 reflect those differences better than general curiosity about technology.

These remain preferences. A fresh graduate may be an experienced gamer; a regular player or practical hobbyist should not read the ads as esports-only. The screenshots show three equally weighted questions, not the entire scoring model.

### 3. Practical fit deserves more space

Location, nights and education appear in every ad, but they are visually subordinate. Move location nearer the main message, enlarge shift information, and split the education line for mobile readability. Remove generic filler or decorative arrows before shrinking essential facts.

Minimum wage affects the decision. The reviews recommend disclosure in primary text and the job page, with the exact amount and pay basis confirmed first. It does not need to become the image headline. The direct-response reviewer favors even earlier disclosure; the copywriter explicitly favors primary text. This is a recommendation, not an applied change or proof of better results.

### 4. Training should be visible on the destination

Training is confirmed by the user and prominent in the ads. The live job page, rechecked during the study, does not mention it. Add that confirmed fact before launch; confirm paid status and duration before making further claims. This is a handoff inconsistency, not evidence that the ad claim is false.

### 5. VR is a test hypothesis

Headsets offer a distinctive visual shape but conceal eye contact and can read as entertainment. Natural-looking portraits improve plausibility without proving actual-workplace authenticity. Retain male and female imagery and a face-visible comparison. No source establishes that VR or any depicted gender will produce better applicants.

## Synthesized shortlist

| Priority | Concept | Why keep it | Proposed next revision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | F1, competitive gamer | Strong current/former experience invitation | Replace “Try robot operations” with a clear hiring invitation; make role/location prominent. |
| 2 | F2, FPS/racing/flight sims | Specific interests that match high-scoring genres | Use “We're hiring Robot Operators” as the bridge; retain VR as another example in primary text. |
| 3 | F3, hands-on controllers | Reaches RC/drone/hardware hobbyists beyond esports | Make clear that RC/drone experience is the applicant's hobby, not confirmed work equipment. |
| 4 | C2, plain Robot Operator | Tests immediate job intent against equipment-led curiosity | Replace “Your next application?” with “Hiring in Alabang”; add a plain duty explanation. |
| 5 | E2, learn to operate robots | Broader training-led invitation with VR visual | Add gaming preference and stronger onsite/job context. |

The order is a creative recommendation, not a measured ranking. If resources only allow three distinct initial concepts, F1, F3 and C2 cover experience identity, practical hobbies and vacancy clarity. F2 is a strong alternate if genre recognition is the main question. Choose test size only after budget and volume are known.

**Reserves:** D2 for face-visible imagery, B2 for training/procedural-work reassurance, F4 for VR familiarity. F1 and F2 are not substitutes for all other audiences. Hold B3/C3 from the first shortlist because generic learning/robotics language can imply a course or engineering role. All other options remain available for comparison.

## Where the reviewers disagree

- The copywriter ranks F2 first; the direct-response reviewer puts F3 ahead of it because practical experience offers a different recruitment route.
- The audience simulation and direct-response reviewer value B2's procedures/training message. The copywriter considers its current layout too text-heavy and generic.
- F1 is the strongest invitation for the competitive personas and a potential self-exclusion cue for regular VR users and noncompetitive hobbyists.
- The human preference favors VR. The reviewers still recommend a face-visible or text-led control; preference is not performance proof.

Preserve these disagreements as test questions rather than averaging them into a fabricated confidence score.

## Recommended next creative pass

Keep the evaluated versions as the study baseline. Create revised shortlisted versions separately:

1. Prominent role plus onsite Alabang line.
2. One specific audience hook.
3. A plain explanation of operating remote-controlled robots from the facility in primary text.
4. Clearly readable full-time, nights and college requirements.
5. Training statement consistent with the job page.
6. Known pay level in primary text and confirmed exact pay on the destination.

Four complete proposed ad-copy packages are in `review-copywriting.md`. They have not been applied to the canvas or published.

## Real-world validation and measurement

- Show shortlisted creatives individually at phone-feed size to actual potential applicants. Ask what job, location, schedule and qualifications they understood before explaining the role. Then give confirmed pay/training facts and ask what changes their interest.
- First test materially different complete concepts. Later test a matched headset-on versus face-visible version with the same copy/layout. Existing D2 versus F1 changes several things and would not isolate the headset's effect.
- Use the available experiment tooling for controlled comparison. A delivery pool containing all 17 with unequal spend is exploratory delivery, not a randomized 17-way test.
- Define a suitable completed applicant before launch: confirmed education, onsite/night availability, informed pay acceptance and recruiter assessment of relevant skills. Do not equate the preference score alone with hire quality.
- Primary outcomes: suitable completed applications and cost per suitable applicant, then interview attendance and hires as they mature. Clicks and starts diagnose the funnel; they do not establish hiring success.
- The questionnaire screenshot shows **Qualified Application threshold Disabled**. Confirm threshold, event delivery and attribution before planning optimization on QualifiedApplication. No setting has been changed during this study.
- Budget, baseline volumes and hiring targets are unknown. They are needed to select test arms, duration, sample needs and stopping rules. Low volume should be reported as inconclusive, not forced into a winner.

## Current evidence and its scope

- [Meta Blueprint: creative diversification](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/page/705435-ad-creative-that-converts-how-to-diversify-your-ads-for-better-results), listed March 2026. Primary public guidance supports variation in images, messages and formats, not a particular VR image or claimed percentage lift.
- [Meta Blueprint: measure creative impact](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/253050-ad-creative-testing-course). Public description supports A/B/lift tests and metrics. Publication date not displayed.
- [Akvile DeFazio, Search Engine Land, 26 August 2026](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-creative-diversity-485751). Practitioner argument for meaningful variation rather than volume. Not controlled recruitment research or a guarantee about Meta's algorithm.
- [Kaiser et al., NIM Marketing Intelligence Review, 2026](https://www.nim.org/en/publications/detail/leaving-insight-to-digital-twins). Synthetic respondent research finds positive bias and compressed variation. Supports early concept screening, not using simulated preference as population evidence.

All public source pages were read. Source notes are in `review-sources.md`. The current-year grounding is a focused evidence check, not a claim to have exhaustively reviewed all 2026 advertising research.
